The coordination tax

Keeping each other posted is a part-time job nobody was hired to do

Picture five product managers and the director they report to. Nobody's slacking — that's the problem. Everyone's running their own lane, so the only way to stay aligned is to keep asking: the standup to hear what changed, the sprint review to show it, the weekly report to write it down for whoever missed both. Each one exists because the last one wasn't enough.

And every update gets produced more than once. You tell the standup, then you type it into the tracker, then you summarize it for the director's rollup. Three renditions of the same fact — and the person who needed it still pings you, because it wasn't in the shape they needed.

Not the fix

The usual answer is a better task board. Fine. But a board only knows what people type into it, and keeping it current is one more standing chore — for a team of five, the setup and grooming costs about as much as the meetings it was supposed to replace.

So the board drifts, and when nobody trusts it anymore, someone calls a meeting to find out what's actually true. Now you're maintaining the tool and having the meeting.

Stay aligned, or stay heads-down. Small teams have always had to pick one — until keeping each other posted takes five conversational minutes, and everyone gets exactly the view they asked for.

How Knavo works

Anyone on the team asks. Everyone answers in minutes. Nobody books a meeting.

01

Anyone sets up a check-in

Not just the boss. A PM prepping a sprint review, a director who wants a Friday rollup — write what you need to know, pick your people, set the cadence.

02

Knavo interviews your team

A five-minute conversation with an agent, on each person's own schedule. It follows up the way a good chief of staff would — progress, blockers, who needs what from whom.

03

Your dashboard does the reporting

Answers land on the dashboard of whoever asked, organized around their questions. The sprint review gets read, not held. The weekly report gets assembled, not written.
